Getting Started

How It Works

No auditions, no contracts, no pressure. Here's exactly what happens from the first phone call to your first time on stage.

Start With a Free Chat →
Our Approach

We Match You — We Don't
Just Schedule You

Most studios put you with whoever has an open slot on Tuesday. We start with a conversation, because the right teacher for a 9-year-old learning her first chords is not the right teacher for a working guitarist chasing bebop.

  1. 01
    Step One

    The Conversation

    Call us, or stop by the shop. We'll spend about fifteen minutes on what you want out of playing — the songs you're chasing, whether you've played before, how much time you realistically have in a week, and what's worked or gone badly for you in the past.

    No cost, no commitment. If we're not the right fit for what you're after, we'll say so and point you somewhere better.
  2. 02
    Step Two

    Your Instructor Match

    Based on that conversation we recommend one or two of our five instructors — matched on genre, teaching style, and temperament, not just calendar availability. Freddy for jazz and theory. Houston for electric and ukulele. Ryan for acoustic, classical and flamenco. Cade for bass and upright. Scott for all-round guitar.

    If the match isn't clicking after a few weeks, tell us. Switching instructors is normal, free, and nobody's feelings get hurt.
  3. 03
    Step Three

    Your First Lesson

    Thirty or sixty minutes, one-on-one. Your instructor works out where you actually are — not where a curriculum says you should be — and you leave with a concrete plan and something specific to practise. Complete beginners typically play a recognisable riff or chord change in that first session.

    Don't own an instrument yet? Use one of ours for the lesson. We'd rather you learn what you like before you spend money.
  4. 04
    Step Four

    Building the Routine

    Most students settle into a weekly slot at the same time each week. Your instructor builds theory in alongside technique, so you understand why a change works rather than only memorising shapes. Every few months you'll step back and look at what's changed — it's easy to miss your own progress week to week.

  5. 05
    Step Five

    Playing for People

    Playing alone in a bedroom and playing in front of people are two different skills. Our student recitals and open-mic nights are low-stakes places to build the second one. Entirely optional — plenty of our students never perform and are perfectly happy. But the ones who do tend to improve fastest.

    See what's coming up on our Events page.
The Practical Details

The Questions Everyone
Asks First

🎒
What to Bring

Your instrument if you have one, and a notebook or your phone for notes. That's genuinely it. We supply picks, tuners, cables and music stands.

⏱️
30 or 60 Minutes?

Younger beginners usually do better with 30. Adults and anyone past the first few months generally get more out of 60 — there's time to actually work through something.

📅
How Often

Weekly is the standard and what we recommend. Every other week works if that's what fits your life — progress is slower but it's real.

🎯
How Long Until…

Recognisable songs in weeks. Comfortable playing in months. It depends far more on practice between lessons than on anything that happens in the room.

Already Playing

Coming In With
Years Behind You

A good portion of our students aren't beginners at all. They're people who've played for a decade, hit a ceiling, and want someone to tell them the truth about what's holding them back.

That process looks different. Step one becomes a playing assessment rather than a chat — bring your instrument, play us something you're happy with and something you're not, and we'll go from there.

✓
Technique repair — undoing habits that are capping your speed or tone
✓
Theory that applies — enough to improvise and write, not to pass an exam
✓
Genre crossover — rock players learning jazz voicings, classical players loosening up
✓
Gig preparation — set building, stagecraft, playing with other people

Common Questions

Before You Book

Is the first conversation really free?
+
Yes. It's a conversation, not a lesson — fifteen minutes on the phone or in the shop so we can work out who you should be learning from. There's no charge and no obligation to book anything afterwards.
Am I locked into a contract or a term?
+
No. There's no term commitment and no cancellation fee. We ask for reasonable notice if you need to pause or stop so we can offer the slot to someone on the waiting list.
What if I don't get on with my instructor?
+
Tell us and we'll move you. It happens, it's nobody's fault, and it costs you nothing. A bad teaching match is the single most common reason people quit an instrument — we'd much rather switch you than lose you.
Can I start mid-year, or do you run in terms?
+
Start whenever. Lessons are one-on-one rather than class-based, so there's no cohort to join and nothing to catch up on. If your instructor's schedule is full we'll tell you honestly what the wait looks like.
Do you teach children? What age?
+
Typically from around age 7, though it depends more on the individual child's attention span and hand size than the number. Come in and we'll give you a straight answer about whether now is the right time or whether waiting a year would be kinder.

Step One Takes Fifteen Minutes

Call the shop, or send us a note and we'll ring you back. No sales pitch — just a conversation about what you want to play.
